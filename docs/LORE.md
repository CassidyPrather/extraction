# Lore

What happened in the valleys, who was there, and how the game tells it.
Everything the player reads (notes, terminals, the driver on the radio,
the lock's epilogue) is held to this file. When the game and this file
disagree, fix whichever one is wrong, and say so in the commit.

The story is told only through the world: papers on tables, screens in
offices, a few staged places, and the driver's voice on the radio. There
are no cutscenes and no narrator. Nobody explains the Zone; people who
were there say what they saw.

## The short version

Twelve autumns ago the ground in the Hollow, a dip in the woods east of
Marrow Creek, began to rise, and one October evening the sky over three
valleys went the wrong colour. That was the first Surge. Five days later
the buses left. The Quarantine Authority sealed the valleys, built a Gate
in each, and set its machines, the **Custodian**, to sweep them clean
"pending review": collect everything, keep everything, until someone
decided what to do with the place. The review was never held. The
Authority went home. The machines did not.

The player is one of the people who come in now on a driver's truck, take
what the machines haven't, and get out before the next Surge. The
Custodian can be recalled: its depots answer to three keys, one per
valley, which the Authority's inspector hid when the review was put off.
The notes lead to them.

## Places

The three valleys are one district in old hill country, joined by a
single road over the hills and a branch railway. Each has a south gate
where the soldiers stood and a Gate (capital G) in its far corner where
the machines come and go.

### Marrow Creek: where it started

A river valley: a village around a well, a post office, a shop, a farm, a
sawmill and logging camp, a pumping station, a bus depot, a rail yard, a
radio mast. The **Hollow** lies east of the village; the Institute's
survey post (a ranger tower on the Hollow's edge) watched it for a summer
before anyone else cared.

- The **well** in the village and the **stone ring** on the eastern hill
  are older than anyone's grandparents. Things left at them go.
- The Authority's valley office was the **post office**.
- The buses left from the **bus depot** at six on the 14th of October.
- The depot key for Marrow, **KEY-M**, went down into the Hollow with the
  survey chief in his lockbox and stayed there.

### Kettle Pass: the mine and the shelter

A valley that climbs to a saddle: Miners' Row, a chapel, the mine and its
office, a quarry high on the east side, a hunting lodge, a pump station,
a level crossing with its gatekeeper's cottage, the pass road. Kettle was
evacuated by train and lorry over the pass, a day after Marrow.

- The inspector locked **KEY-K** in the **quarry blast shelter**, with the
  shot-firer's own key. Within a month the shelter "burned without fire"
  and the quarry became a place the stones grow.
- The chapel's **font** and the **cairn** on the west slope take
  offerings, as the well does.

### Cinder Works: the fire

A flat industrial valley: the works under its chimney, a canal with one
bridge and its pumps, slag heaps, Ash Row, a farm, a fire lookout in the
woods, the woodward's cabin, the north sidings.

- Over the first winter the Authority paid the works to burn what the
  machines hauled out of all three valleys. Cars, furniture, bedding.
  The furnace never went cold. In February the fire got out into the
  north-west wood and burned for a month. Ash still falls; the works
  people say it comes up out of the ground now, by the slag heaps.
- **KEY-C** is in a strongbox at the foot of the chimney. The inspector
  left it there himself, the foreman watching.
- The **memorial plinth** in Ash Row (its bronze long gone to the
  furnace) and the **burnt chapel** in the woods take offerings.

### Sallow Reservoir: the water

Upstream of the three, over the hills: the Authority's water works. A
masonry dam across a hill valley, the draw-off tower out on the bed with
its bridge to the crest, the valve house at the dam's toe, the keeper's
cottages and the filter house below, the catchwater and its gauge house
on the east side. The reservoir filled the three valleys' taps.

- **Old Sallow** was drowned when the water board built the dam, sixty-odd
  years before the Lift. **New Sallow** was built up the upper arm for the
  people moved out; it went on the Marrow buses with everyone else ("we
  were moved for the water once already").
- **The drain**: in December of the second winter the Custodian opened the
  scour valves. Collect everything meant what was under the water too.
  The level came down a hand a day for a month and the old village came
  up: church tower, bridge, house walls. The upper arm, behind the
  causeway, never drained; its culverts silted shut and it rose over New
  Sallow's lower street.
- **The stock ground**: with the depots full, the machines keep stock on
  the bed in rows, tagged "pending review": buses (Marrow depot's, by
  number; bus four isn't there), bicycles in racks, bins in ranks.
- Its Gate on the north-east shore is an overflow store under the Marrow
  depot. There is no fourth key; the recall reaches it.
- The **drowned font** by the church tower and **the village pump** in New
  Sallow take offerings.
- **Alois Pech**, the keeper, was seven when they moved his family up the
  hill. He stayed with the dam at the evacuation, watched his village come
  back up, and kept reading the catchwater gauge on Sundays until his legs
  gave out.

### Rime Fell: the mine above the trees

The district's high valley, over the fells to the north-west: a glacial
bench above the treeline and the mine company's settlement on it,
**Coldharbour** (lead, zinc, a little silver from the knoll). One street,
the mine office, the canteen, a chapel, and the funicular down to the
valley station, the only way out in winter. The stepped ore mill on the
knoll, the observatory on the summit, the galleries over the pass road.

- Coldharbour came down the funicular on the 15th, the last car at four.
  The two at the **observatory** (the weather service's high station and
  the district's radio relay), **Ota Brož** and **Hedvika Šimková**,
  stayed: someone had to say what came over the col. Their entries are
  in the col hut book and the avalanche watch's log.
- The Gate is in the cirque past the tarn; the machines came over the col
  on 2 November in a line. They want the mine: the concentrate, the
  rails, the rope. In March the funicular's rope was cut at the top
  drum and wound away; one car stands at the passing loop. On 4 November
  the adit door was opened, with no card.
- The **crown hole**: the old workings fell in the first winter and the
  ground sank into a pit. Things come up out of it. The **miners' shrine**
  by the chapel and **the cairn on the col** take offerings.

## Who and what

### The Custodian (the machines)

The Authority's automated sweep: it was built to make three valleys safe
by removing everything from them and keeping it until the review. Its
terminals call it the **Custodian network**; people in the valleys call
them the white machines. It is not cruel and not curious. It is doing a
job nobody told it to stop.

- **Units**: survey (the little flying drones, the airships that drop
  pods), haul (grapplers carrying stock), build (walkers, which also
  fight), recover (the collector, which comes for the dead). **Pylons**
  are its eyes: each reports what moves near it, and the depot sends a
  team.
- **Sweep**: collect loose material, take it through the Gate, keep it.
  It took the bins, the bicycles, the buses. It took Mr Holm.
- **Personnel**: anyone the pylons report is uncleared personnel in a
  quarantined valley, and the machines treat you as material that fights
  back. A body is "recovered personnel": the collector takes it, and
  everything on it, through the Gate "pending review". The driver calls
  that drive the **memorial convoy**, and keeps the count in the truck.
- **The Gates** are its depots, one per valley. Nobody who went through
  one has come back to say what's inside.
- **The keys**: a depot answers only to all three valley keys presented
  together, so that no single office could stop the sweep alone. The
  Authority meant to bring them together at the review. Presented at any
  depot's lock, they recall every machine in every valley (the capstone).

### The Surge

The Authority's timetable calls it a "field discharge", scheduled every
cycle, that "clears contamination", as if it were theirs. It isn't. It
came first, on the 9th of October, before any machine; the Authority
wrote it into the sweep schedule afterwards, and the Custodian works
around it. After the recall it still comes. Nobody knows what it is.

What people say: the sky goes the wrong colour; the air hums, then tastes
of pennies; anyone in the open dies, anyone under a roof aches for a day.
Afterwards the valley is different: things moved, things gone, the stones
grown somewhere new.

### The stones (artifacts)

"Bright stones", to the people who stayed; "field-active material" to the
Authority. Each kind of wrong ground grows its own, and they are gone at
the next Surge if no one takes them. The scavengers' names: a lift grows
a **featherstone**, a burner a **hearth**, a snare an **aegis knot**, a
well a **lodestone**, a mirror a **mirror shard**. A **glass eye** turns
up anywhere, rarely. Rarest is the **stormglass**, which grows only where
the Surge itself breaks the ground, about one a Surge, in its last
minutes.

Each does something odd and useful while carried, and each costs you
something in the body:

| Stone | What it asks of you | How it's got out (what they call it) |
| - | - | - |
| Featherstone | It wants home: lifts you pass wind up and pop | "Let it throw": it rides the column until something winds the lift up and it flings the stone |
| Hearth | Runs you hot; your wind comes back slowly | "Wait for the coals": too hot to hold until it cools or is quenched |
| Aegis knot | Holds a charge and bites when wet | "Earth it first": safe only once something metal has drawn the charge |
| Lodestone | Far heavier on you than in the hand | "Go to the heart": it hangs at the crushing middle of the well |
| Mirror shard | Its reflection runs late, and glints where anything can see | "Catch the fold": reachable as the mirror closes after something's gone through |
| Stormglass | The Surge passes over you as if under a roof; the whole Zone knows where it is | Taken from the Surge's own ground, in its last minutes |

Each also **hums**, and the machines hear it. "If you hear the hum,
you're carrying too many of the bright stones. Put some down." A
lead-lined case quiets the hum, the cost and the gift together.

### The offering places

The well, the stone ring, the chapel font, the cairn, the memorial plinth,
the burnt chapel. They take what's left in them and, once in a while,
answer: a calm hour, a whisper of where a stone lies, the Surge held off.
Nobody in the notes claims to know why. The pump keeper thinks the well
was like this before the Lift, just slower.

Most take any stone alike. A few answer one stone in their own way
(`src/anomaly/offering.rs`):

| Place | Stone | Answer |
| - | - | - |
| The well (Marrow) | Stormglass | The Surge held off far longer: "the well drinks the storm" |
| The cairn (Kettle) | Lodestone | Refused: the stone rolls back out at your feet |
| The burnt chapel (Cinder) | Hearth | Taken gladly: a calm hour, the machines slow |
| The chapel font (Kettle) | Mirror shard | A whisper: where the nearest stone lies |

### The driver

**Lev Marek**, in his fifties. Born in Marrow Creek. On the 14th of October
he drove bus number four out of the depot at six, full, and told his
mother, **Hana**, it would be three days; that's what they'd told the
drivers to say. She stayed behind for the dog. An hour later he turned the
empty bus round at the south gate and asked the soldiers to let him back
in for her; they wouldn't. He has never been past the fence since. He
won't say he's afraid of it.

He drives people in now, and out, and keeps their count. He is dry,
practical, generous in small ways, and talks about himself only sideways.
His sister **Anna** lives in town and is ill (the dressings he asks for
"aren't for him"). Hana's letter to Anna is still on the table in the
house by the well, in Marrow; it says she has written to Lev as well. The
last of his jobs (`src/progression`) sends you for that second letter,
sealed: "For Lev, when he comes back for me." He reads it alone.

He's in the cab, never in the bed: he doesn't come where you've been.
What he gives you he passes back through a hatch in the bed's front wall
(`src/raid/lev.rs`): it slides open, his hand comes through with the
thing (his old carbine, the gun from his man in town, his mother's
photograph), sets it on the ledge, and goes back. And on the drive home
he notices what you've brought: a stone humming in your kit, one of their
eyes, a cell, a week of tins.

The game reveals him slowly, a line at a time on the drive home
(`src/raid/lines.rs`): first the job, then the bus, then the gate, then
his mother. Notes in all three valleys mention him without his name;
only his mother's letter names him.

## The thread

Notes refer to each other across valleys, and every key is named in at
least two notes, at least one of them in another valley. In the order a
player is likely to meet them:

1. **Who the driver is.** The Marrow south gate log (bus four turned
   back), Hana's letter (Lev drove the four), the scavenger's note in
   Cinder (he won't come past the fence; he tried once).
2. **What the machines are and want.** The depot office terminal, the
   relay logs, Ondrej's note (the Surge was here first), the chapel
   (the machine that cleaned the windows).
3. **The keys.** The undelivered letter to Vey (Marrow), Vey's last log
   at the survey post (he takes the box into the Hollow), Marta's note
   (the box is still down there); Irena's note and Karel's (Kettle: the
   shelter); Ruben's log (Cinder: the chimney); and Rus's own last
   letter, left in the works garage, which names all three places and
   says why he hid them.
4. **The recall.** The depot terminals say what three keys do. The lock
   says it again. The epilogue on the lock answers "for whoever comes
   after".

`src/world/story.rs` holds the thread as data (which note mentions which
place), and its tests keep every mention pointing at a real place and
every note on the thread.

## The arc: Hana's thread

One thread runs the length of a career and ends at the recall: what
happened to Lev's mother. Each rank of the driver's job ladder
(`docs/PROGRESSION.md`) opens one beat. A beat is physical: a scene with
a note that appears somewhere new in Marrow (`after:` in
`assets/data/scenes.ron`), a few lines from Lev on the radio between raids
(`raid::lines::BEAT_*`), sometimes something he hands over. Nobody says
what it adds up to; each note names the one before, so the player can
piece it together. `world::story::BEATS` holds the list, and its tests
hold it to the ladder.

## The Depot

Under the Marrow Gate (Underground's `depot_marrow`) is where the convoys
go: intake, the sorting hall's bins (the bicycles, the bins, the buses),
and the kept stacks. Nothing is destroyed; everything is kept, tagged,
pending a review that was never held. In bay K-14, off the stacks, are
the Mareks: a long closed crate with her basket strapped on the lid, a
small one with RUSTY on the collar tag, and Jan Holm's beside them; Vey is
in the middle row, "recovered: the Hollow", with his notebook. The tag
office has the intake terminal and the review docket Rus prepared and
nobody stamped. The crates stay shut; the game never opens them. After
the recall the machines sleep in their bays and the terminal says
"Release procedure: NOT FOUND": nothing in them knows how to give
anything back.

## The ending

The recall stops the machines; it doesn't end the story. At the recall
Lev says he's coming next time, and leaves a note on a crate by the
south gate where the soldiers turned bus four back. On the next raid in
Marrow he steps down from the cab and walks behind you (`src/raid/
epilogue.rs`), all the way to the Gate and down the intake ramp. Nothing
moves down there now. At the tag office the intake terminal finds a
release procedure at last ("Review: closed (recall). Kept material is
released to next of kin on request. Request: MAREK, L."), the kept crates
open, and when the stacks are lit he walks to bay K-14 and stands by her
crate: "Mum." Rusty is in the small one; he takes the collar home. "Go
on up. I'll sit with her a while." You leave when you like; the raid
ends at the truck as any does, he's back in the cab, and on the drive
he thanks you. After that his chair stands by the well, facing her
house, with his note: her found, the collar on the hook with the lead.
It's played once a career (`Stats::epilogue`); the score is silent for
it.

## The board

A cork board on the truck's right canopy wall, over the first-aid box, is
where the player pieces it together (`src/raid/board.rs`). Every note read
(looked at from within arm's reach for a moment) gets a pinned copy with
its name, in a column for its valley, in the order read; where two read
notes name each other in the thread (`world::story::THREAD`), red string
runs pin to pin. Nothing unread shows: no gaps, no blank cards, so the
board never says what's missing. Press a card to take the note down and
read it whole; press again to pin it back. The read notes are kept with
the career (`Stats::notes_read`). The first note read, Lev says he keeps
copies (`onboarding::firsts::First::NotePinned`, said once a career).

## Staged scenes

Small arrangements of existing props and items at the points of interest
(`assets/data/scenes.ron`, spawned by `src/world/scenes.rs`), each a
moment someone didn't come back from. A scene should be readable without
its note; a note, if it has one, adds a fact, not an explanation.

story by what's left and rewarding whoever looks closely (`Prop` pieces
draw any model from `props/`). What's left to take lies on something,

## Tone rules

- **Understated.** Nobody says "horror", "nightmare" or "doomed". Fear is
  shown by what people did: stayed, locked a door, wrote it down.
- **Specific.** A time, a name, a number, an object. "They took the
  bicycles" beats "they took everything".
- **Never jokey.** Dry humour is allowed when a person would use it to
  get through a day (Lev, Hana). No winks at the player, no game terms
  (no "loot", "quest", "spawn", "respawn", "HP").
- **No exposition dumps.** A note is one person on one day. If a note
  explains the Zone, cut the explanation.
- **People don't know everything.** Notes disagree a little, guess, and
  are sometimes wrong (the Authority about the Surge). The lore here is
  the truth; the notes are testimony.
- **Plain words, short sentences, British spelling.** Machine text is in
  capitals, clipped, and never says "please".
- **Readable in the headset.** Notes fit their paper with letters at
  least `MIN_EM_DEG` at a metre (in practice under about 450 characters);
  terminal pages at most 32 columns by 12 lines; anything said on the
  radio fits the wrist's three lines of 26. Tests hold all three.
