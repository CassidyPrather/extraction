# Coming home: the ways in to the rendezvous

The other walk every raid has: back to the truck, laden, late, with the
valley awake. It should be tense but fair, never a gauntlet. Walked by
the coming-home half of `docs/craft/WALK_REVIEW.md`, in each valley's
reference weather.

The measure is the map lint's coming-home rule. It takes the last 150 m
into each rendezvous from every place that goes home there, and counts
the lines in and whether there's a second way with the busiest shut. It
also finds the longest run with nothing to get behind a dash (12 m) off,
and how much of the way a spotter, a survey drone or a walker at its
post watches. It flags a run over 24 m (five seconds at a run) and a
rendezvous with one way in.

The Verifier's smoke that started this (two deaths on the way home to
Kettle's Mine yard, at (-202, -40) and (-194, -38)) ran Kettle as it was
before the pull-in, with the rendezvous at (-205, -30). Pulled in,
Mine yard has three lines in and nothing longer than 14 m in the open:
it isn't the gauntlet it was.

How much is watched is in the lint's measures, not its rules. Survey
drones' circles cover most of every valley's last 150 m, because the
points of interest are everywhere and a drone surveys only one at a
time. The number says where a drone could be, not where it is.

## The fixes

The place's own cover, at the spacing the ground gives, beside the way
and out of the views on the way in: never a row of identical woodpiles.
Each pair is the same camera, at the start of the run, looking home.

### Marrow Creek

**Rail yard, from the Hollow.** The climb up the Ford track to the crest
was a grass bank with nothing on it. Now an outcrop stands on the inside
of the bend, on the hill's flank: something to make for, then the
crest's view of the yard.

### Sallow Reservoir

Sallow is open by design: the reservoir, its silt fans and its shores.
Its ways home were the worst, up to 100 m of wet mud with nothing on it.
They now have birch and willow copses on the slopes, outcrops over the
silt fans and above the old road, and woodpiles by the water and on the
slope down to the Inflow bridge. There's also a car ditched on the west
shore.

The west shore taught a lesson: its way runs on a strip a few metres
wide between a bank and the water. An outcrop on the bank sank into it,
and one on the strip shut Sallow Head's second way in. The woodpile
stands between the road and the water's edge.

## The approaches

Each rendezvous's main way in at 150, 100, 50 and 10 m out, before (upper
row) and after (lower), three rendezvous to a valley. The 150 m stop
should already say "home is that way", and the last 50 m should show the
beacon and somewhere to get down. In every valley they do. The cover
mostly stands beside the way, off these frames.
